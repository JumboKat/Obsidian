### Instability in the Balkans
![[Pasted image 20260528200945.png]]Having controlled the Balkans for centuries, the Ottoman Empire was now losing its grip on the region. As the empire declined and nationalism among the Balkan peoples grew stronger, new independent states emerged, including Montenegro, Greece, Serbia, Romania, and Bulgaria, though through much violence and political turmoil. 

After defeating the Ottomans in the **Russo-Turkish War** in 1878, Russia had positioned itself as the protector of the Balkans and the guarantor of the independence of several Balkan states, though the other European powers were concerned about Russia's influence over the region. In 1878, Austria-Hungary occupied Bosnia and Herzegovina, and at the **Berlin Conference** in 1884, the great powers returned most of Macedonia to the Ottomans.

![[Pasted image 20260528202435.png]]
By the early twentieth century, tensions among the great powers, who wanted stability in the region, and between the Balkan states, who were now looking to expand their territories, were high. Austria-Hungary wanted to stem the influence of Serbia, who was seeking influence over the South Slavs living in the empire. Serbia was now a close ally of Russia, who wanted a strong ally in the region as well as access to the Turkish straits.

Having lost several wars and forced to give up many of its territories, the declining Ottoman Empire was wracked by political turmoil from within. 1908 saw the [[Young Turk Revolution]] in July, bringing a new constitution and a focus on modernizing the military, a strong Turkish identity, and preserving the empire's remaining territories, Macedonia in particular. The situation was gravely complicated in October when Austria-Hungary formally annexed Bosnia and Herzegovina, introducing even more tension to the Balkans. This lead to a chaotic political response in Constantinople, with an attempt to overthrow the new constitution, a counter-coup, another constitution, and a new sultan, **Mehmet V**. The next year saw the loss of formal control over Crete, and a revolt in Albania backed by Montenegro. The Balkan states also suffered political instability, with a struggle to reign in their nationalist militaries. 

![[Pasted image 20260530121622.png]]
In 1911, with the start of the [[Italo-Turkish War (1911-1912)||Italo-Turkish War]], the Balkan states took advantage of the situation, and Serbia, Bulgaria, Montenegro and Greece formed the **Balkan League**. Together, they sought to take back Balkan lands from Ottoman hands. This did not come without complications, however, as all parties held conflicting claims over which territories belonged to them, claims which most of the time did not align with the mixed ethnic composition of the local populations. With no clear plan as to how to distribute the territory, they proceeded anyway with their plan to wage war against the Ottoman Empire. 
### Outbreak of War
On October 8th, 1912, Montenegro attacked the Ottomans ahead of schedule, with the other members giving the Ottomans an ultimatum. While Grand Vizier **Ahmed Muhtar Pasha** wanted to keep the peace, the influential Young Turks party called for war. With the empire already on the brink of civil war, the Ottomans declared war on the Balkan League on October 16th.

![[Pasted image 20260530122237.png]]
Most European observers expected that the much larger Ottoman Empire would come out victorious. On paper, they could field 600,000 troops, many of which had gained experience fighting in the [[Italo-Turkish War (1911-1912)|Italo-Turkish War]]. However, the Ottoman military was fraught with issues; its navy was greatly weakened, with many of their best military leaders still stuck in Libya. Overall, the Ottomans only had 315,000 men available in Europe, while the Balkan League could muster a total of 825,000, most of which were peasant conscripts equipped with a variety of European-made weaponry. The Greek navy boasted 16 destroyers and an armoured cruiser, ensuring naval dominance in the **Aegean Sea**. The Great Powers stated on October 10th that they would see to it that the status quo be maintained, though in actuality, they planned only to prevent the Ottomans from gaining territory should the war play out in their favour. 
### Thracian Campaign
![[Pasted image 20260530163010.png]]
The Bulgarians would fight most of their important battles in Eastern Thrace. The Ottomans, believing the Bulgarians would be most interested in Macedonia, moved the bulk of their forces there. The Bulgarians sent three armies to attack and surround the Ottoman First Army at the fortress towns of **Edirne** (**Adrianople**) and **Kirk Kilisse**. At the Battle of Kirk Kilisse, the Ottoman commander Abdullah Pasha, unaware that his army was outnumbered, sent his troops in a hasty advance on the town on October 21st. After three days, the Bulgarians broke the Ottoman lines, but did not take the opportunity to pursue them, allowing the Ottomans to garner reinforcements and dig in at **Lule Burgas**. On October 29th, the Bulgarians attacked them, and defeated them with superior artillery power in the largest battle of Europe between the Franco-Prussian War and the First World War. 

![[Pasted image 20260530163801.png]]
The Ottomans retreated to the **Çatalca Line** on November 2nd, just 30km away from Constantinople. The Bulgarians, refusing an armistice request by the Ottomans, tried to break through the line on November 17th, but fierce resistance and a cholera outbreak stopped their advance. While the Bulgarians were not ultimately able to break through, their advances in Thrace, coupled alongside the Greek navy off the coast, meant that the rest of the Ottomans in the Balkans were now isolated. Many ethnic Bulgarian residents of the captured towns welcomed the Bulgarian troops as liberators.
### Macedonian Campaign
![[Pasted image 20260530165320.png]]
In Macedonia, the Serbians were attacked at **Kumanovo** on October 23rd. Through rain and mud, the Serbs made costly counterattacks, which observers had compared to those of the Japanese during the [[Russo-Japanese War (1904-1905)|Russo-Japanese War]]. Ultimately, the Serbians persevered with the aid of superior artillery, and were victorious at the Battle of Kumanovo. They now held the region of Thrace disputed with the Bulgarians, and sent the Ottomans fleeing south.

The Serbians pursued the Ottomans, beating them back at the Battles of Prilep and Bitola, forcing them to withdraw further into southern Albania. The Serbians also make a push towards **Scutari** and the Adriatic coast, joining up with Montenegrins in besieging the town. They also captured the **Sanjak of Novi Pazar** and the town of **Prizren**, which the Montenegrins had wanted. Finally, the Serbians made an advance into Kosovo, but faced resistance from the local Albanians. The Greeks, wanting to assert their claim before the Bulgarians, surrounded the city of **Salonika** on November 7th. The Bulgarians rushed south, asking the Ottoman commander to surrender to them instead of the Greeks, but by then they had already surrendered to the Greeks on November 8th. Afterward, both nations began an uneasy joint presence in the city. At the same time, the Greeks besieged the Ottomans at **Yanina**. 
### Atrocities and Migrations
During the war, both Christians and Muslims suffered atrocities committed by all parties. Many locals supported their countrymen's armies, which blurred the distinction between civilians and soldiers. Many Christians turned on oppressive Muslim officials, and seized lands from Muslim and even Christian landowners. The Greeks destroyed many Muslim villages on the claim that they had been shot at by Muslims. In many cases, all of the village men were executed.

A wave of Muslim refugees, numbering in the hundreds of thousands, poured into Constantinople. The famous **Blue Mosque** was turned into a refugee camp, and the **Hagia Sofia** into a cholera hospital. All over the Balkans, the Islamic population fled as they were persecuted and their homelands settled by their Christian neighbors. Warships were sent to Constantinople by the Great Powers to prevent any reprisals against the city's Christian population.
### November Armistice and Great Power Intervention
The Balkan League had asserted near-complete control over the region, with just a few Ottoman holdouts still fighting. On November 28th, Albania declared independence, and on December 3rd, the Ottomans agreed to an armistice with Bulgaria, Montenegro, and Serbia, though Greece still remained in the fight. The Ottomans were not yet defeated the armistice, and still held defensive positions at key fortresses and the Çatalca Line. However, they would not receive support from the Great Powers as they had in the past, who this time would now accept cessation of territory to the Balkan League.

Additionally, the conflict was close to drawing Europe into a larger war. On November 21st, Austria-Hungary had mobilized six army corps to prevent Serbia from occupying the Adriatic coast, three of which faced Russia. Just as he would do again during the [[July Crisis]] in 1914, **Kaiser Wilhelm** secretly assured the Austrians that Germany would provide military support should the Russians mobilize for war. In response to Austrian mobilization, Russia held a war council meeting and drew up plans for partial mobilization, but ultimately decided against it in fear of provoking German involvement. A similar event occurred in Germany on December 8th, with mobilization just barely avoided.

On December 16th and 17th, 1912, two parallel conferences took place in London, one involving the Balkan League and the Ottoman Empire, and one involving the European Powers. At the first, the Ottomans agreed to some but not all of the territorial demands of the League, and supported an independent Albania rather than dividing it among the Serbs, Montenegrins, and Greeks. They denied the Bulgarian request for Edirne/Adrianople, citing the fortress city's importance in the security of Constantinople. The Greeks and Bulgarians argued over Salonika, while the Serbs and Bulgarians argued over Macedonia. At the separate conference, the main discussion surrounded the borders of Albania, which the Austrians wanted to use to limit Serbia's access to the Adriatic.
### Renewed Ottoman Campaign
On January 23rd, 1913, the Young Turks party seized control of the government after a military coup and the murder of War Minister **Nazim Pasha**. With support from many military officers like **Enver Bey**, who's homelands were now lost to the Balkan League, they decided to continue the fight in an attempt to reclaim Thrace. On February 7th, the Ottomans landed at Gallipoli and attacked the Bulgarians but were brutally repulsed, with 6000 Ottomans dead to the Bulgarians 114. On March 6th, Yanina fell to the Greeks, and on March 26th, Edirne was captured by the Serbians and Bulgarians. The Montenegrins and Serbians assaulted Scutari at the behest of the Great Powers. A combined fleet by the Powers blockaded Montenegro, causing the Serbs to withdraw their support, but the Montenegrins captured the city anyway on April 24th, though they agreed to hand it over to the future independent Albania. With their now total defeat in the Balkans, the Ottomans were forced to accept a peace deal.
### Treaty of London
![[Pasted image 20260530173336.png]]
The **Treaty of London** was signed on May 30th, 1913, ending the **First Balkan War**. It reduced Ottoman territory in Europe to a small parcel of land just outside Constantinople, and ratified the existence of Albania. 

![[Pasted image 20260530173602.png]]
However, the new borders were still in dispute among the members of the League, and Romania now demanded the region of **Southern Dobrudja**. In May 1913, the Great Powers decided that Romania would gain the town of Silistra, which satisfied neither side and cast doubt among Bulgarians about Russia's reliability as an ally. Bulgaria also felt wronged, having won the most strategically important battles in Thrace, and yet Serbia had gained most of Macedonia and did not want to honour their pre-war agreement. Bulgaria was also in conflict with Greece over Salonika. 

Russia tried to mediate an agreement between Serbia and Bulgaria with little success. They also did not want Bulgarian troops so close to Constantinople, fearing they would take it before the Russians could. Bulgaria was in a precarious situation, as many of its soldiers were now exhausted from the war, and were willing to compromise on the Macedonian issue in favour of peace.
### The Second Balkan War
Fearing the loss of Macedonia for good, **Tsar Ferdinand I of Bulgaria** ordered an attack on Serbia and Greece on June 29th. The Bulgarian Prime Minister tried to stop the fighting, but was too late. With Bulgaria as the aggressor, the Serbs and Greeks agreed to split Macedonia between just the two of them, with Montenegro joining in support of Serbia.

![[Pasted image 20260530175109.png]]
The initial attack was uncoordinated and not well-communicated. The Serbs halted their advance and defeated the Bulgarians at **Brgrelnica** by July 8th. Around the same time, the Greeks defeated the Bulgarians around **Kilkis**, **Doiran** and Salonika. The Serbs and Greeks met up on July 11th in a combined front against the Bulgarians. Greek, Turkish, and Serbian civilians were attacked by Bulgarians during their retreat, and Bulgarian civilians suffered at the hands of advancing Serbians and Greeks on the basis of claims of attacks against them.
#### Romanian and Ottoman Entry
On July 10th, Romania seized the opportunity to take Southern Dobrudja. 250,000 Romanian troops advanced into Bulgaria and towards the capital of **Sofia**. While Bulgarian resistance was unorganized, the Romanians were plagued by a cholera epidemic due to lack of medical care and unsanitary conditions.

Seeing an opportunity to recover Thrace, the Ottomans crossed the Çatalca Line on July 12th, brushing past scattered Bulgarian resistance and capturing Edirne on July 23rd without firing a shot. With the Romanians so close to Sofia and no support from Russia, the pro-Russian government resigned, with a pro-German government taking its place.

The Bulgarians continued the fight, and repelled the Serbs and Montenegrins at **Kalimantsi** and successfully counterattacked the Greeks at **Kresna Gorge**. The Greeks called for an armistice, and Bulgaria knew that no victory could reverse the tide of the war.
#### Treaties of Bucharest and Constantinople
![[Pasted image 20260530180602.png]]
On August 10th, 1913, Bulgaria signed the **Treaty of Bucharest** with Serbia, Greece, and Montenegro, and on September 29th, 1913, signed the **Treaty of Constantinople** with the Ottoman Empire. Both treaties saw the loss of Bulgarian territory to its enemies. Romania got Southern Dobrudja; Serbia got most of Macedonia, but Bulgaria retained some of it due to the lack of support from Austria-Hungary and Russia for Serbia's demands. Greece kept Salonika, with the Ottomans regaining Eastern Thrace. 
### Aftermath
![[Pasted image 20260530180909.png]]
The Balkan Wars had devastated the region, with all parties sustaining heavy losses. The Ottomans' 600-year presence in Europe was now reduced to a fraction of its size. Albania was now independent, though faced border disputes from its neighbors. Many Balkan Christians saw the outcome as the end of foreign oppression, while over 300,000 Balkan Muslims were expelled from their homes and were now forced to resettle elsewhere in the empire, bringing much political and religious angst against Christians in the empire. Enver Pasha, whose homeland was now in control of Balkan peoples, would later play a role in the [[Armenian Genocide]] and killing of Greeks in the First World War.

The outcome of the Balkan Wars would create the conditions that would foment the [[July Crisis||July Crisis of 1914]]. Bulgaria would drift closer to Austria-Hungary and Germany due to the lack of support from Russia, and tensions between Austria-Hungary and the now more powerful Serbia was rising. With Serbia now being Russia's only remaining ally in the Balkans, Russia would be under more pressure to support them in any future conflict. In October 1913, Austria with German support forced Serbia to remove its troops from Northern Albania via an ultimatum, a move similar to the one that would one day start the First World War.
