The touchscreen display is a revolutionary piece of technology that paved the way for the ubiquitous modern smartphone. It is the product of a seamless integration of three different technologies: toughened glass, a capacitive touchscreen, and an LCD/OLED display.

The first computer with a touchscreen was the HP-150, commercialized in 1983 by the HP Company. 
### Chemically Toughened Glass
![[Pasted image 20260519110210.png]]
The glass of a standard smartphone is over 5x stronger than regular glass. Before the introduction of the iPhone in 2007, a standard cellphone screen was made of plastic. Although plastic is much more shatterproof, it is very easily scratched—it would be covered by dozens of scratches simply from daily use—and is much less aesthetically-pleasing.

![[Pasted image 20260519131223.png]]
The glass used in a smartphone is an aluminosilicate glass that is toughened by soaking it in molten (400C) potassium nitrate. This process expels sodium atoms in the glass and replaces them with much larger potassium atoms. Due to their larger size, they exert a substantial compressive force on the surface of the glass; the atoms are compressed, requiring much more force to break the glass.
### Projected Capacitive Touchscreen
Below the glass sits a capacitive touchscreen that is sensitive to capacitive materials such as one's fingertip.

![[Pasted image 20260519131558.png]]The touchscreen is multilayered, with two transparent diamond grids printed on polyester with a transparent insulator in between. The diamond grid pattern is printed with **Indium Tin Oxide** (**ITO**), which acts as a conductor. Optically, the top and bottom grids interlock, so that the holes in the top grid are filled by the bottom grid.

![[Pasted image 20260519141048.png|697]]
The diamonds of the bottom grid (blue) are connected in rows, forming "drive" lines. The diamonds of the top grid (yellow) are connected in columns, forming "sense" lines. Electrons are kept stationary by the insulator. Voltage is sent through the drive lines, inducing electrons to create a negative electrostatic field at each diamond, causing positive charges to build up on the top grid, forming a [[capacitor]]. 
![[Pasted image 20260519141416.png|335]]
When a conductive material (such as the tip of a finger) moves close to the capacitor, it siphons some of the charge, as the body acts as a ground. changing the amount of positive charges on the yellow diamond. The decrease in capacitance at that node is measured and registered as a touch. Measuring each node requires too much circuitry, so only each column is measured. The voltage is sent to each row in quick succession, allowing multiple touches to be registered at once.
### OLED Display
Below the capacitive touchscreen lies the display, responsible for generating the high-quality images on the cellphone. The display can be a **liquid crystal display (LCD)** or an **organic light emitting diode (OLED)** display, the latter of which is the standard of most new smartphones.

![[Pasted image 20260519143235.png]]
High-end phones can have over 3.3 million pixels. OLED displays are essentially a grid of individual pixels, with each pixel comprised of a red, green, and blue subpixel. The light intensity of each subpixel is controlled by a thin film transistor that acts as a dimmer switch.

![[Pasted image 20260519143517.png]]
To produce photons in each subpixel, electrons are driven from a negative terminal to a positive terminal and pass through the **emissive layer** on the way. When they pass through this layer, they emit photons as a release of energy. The compounds used to make up the emissive layer determines the colour of the light emitted, and the amount of electrons driven through it determine the light's intensity.
