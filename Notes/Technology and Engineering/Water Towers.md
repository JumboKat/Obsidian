### Water Transport
Most water systems use pipes to transport water. While open channels have been and still are used, there are many disadvantages associated with their use:
1. Water is at risk of contamination.
2. Water channels must run overground, bisecting the land and becoming an obstacle.
3. Since they use gravity to move water, they can only transport it to areas of lower elevation.
Using pipes solves all of these issues, as they are enclosed and can use pressure to move fluids. As fluids move from high to low pressure, making one end of the pipe high pressure allows you to send the water outward. 
### Pumps
Pumps are used to create the high pressure needed to move water through pipes. However, pumps can be expensive, and are rate-limited. When serving a large population, who's demand for water varies greatly throughout the day, the pump runs at low efficiency during the night, but can be overwhelmed at peak hours (etc. in the morning). Using a pump alone, this would require investing in a higher capacity pump just to serve peak hours, while its level of operation during other hours would remain the same.
### Water Towers
![[Pasted image 20260704130742.png]]
Using a water tower alongside a pump can solve the issue of demand without needing a more expensive pump. During low hours, the pump fulfills the low demand, with its excess capacity used to fill the water tower. During peak hours, when the pump cannot singlehandedly meet demand, the water tower is drained to cover the excess. This system ensures that a pump can run at full efficiency during all times of the day, while able to withstand demand spikes.

Water towers are also instrumental for emergencies such as fires. Municipalities have minimum requirements for stored water available for firefighters.
#### Creating Water Pressure
Water towers are elevated in order to store the potential energy used to pressurize water. This way, they can store both water and the energy required to move it. Pumps fill them up at night when electricity is cheap, and are sometimes turned off during the day (when electricity is more expensive) to allow the water tower alone to pressurize the water. Having the water elevated also allows water to still be pressurized during power outages, when pumps are out of service.
#### Elevation Challenges
![[Pasted image 20260704131808.png]]
In most cases, consumers will be at different elevations with respect to the water tower, and so will experience different levels of water pressure. Those at low elevation may experience too much pressure, which can damage plumbing; some houses have special taps to reduce pressure for this reason. Those who are at high elevation will have low pressure. Many large cities use different **pressure zones**; separate areas of elevation that are served with their own water tower.

![[Pasted image 20260704132149.png]]
Tall buildings like skyscrapers will have many consumers of different elevations within the same area. To solve this issue, most high-rise buildings have their own pumps, with some cities like New York requiring them to have their own water tower.
