Pin tumbler locks are the most common form of lock. Every key has a unique shape, and each lock can only be unlocked by a specific matching key.
### External Anatomy
![[Pasted image 20260527103342.png]]
On the outside, the lock consists of a **plug** surrounded by the **case** (gray). The hole is known as the **keyway**.
### Internals
![[Pasted image 20260527103642.png|457]]
Inside are vertical shafts that penetrate the case and the plug, each consisting of a **spring**, a **driver pin**, and a **key pin**. 

![[Pasted image 20260527103841.png|626]]
The tensile springs push the shafts down so that they rest on the bottom ledge. The driver and key pins in each shaft meet at different elevations. 

![[Pasted image 20260527104208.png|696]]![[Pasted image 20260527104303.png]]
To unlock the lock, the pins must all line up with the **shear line**. When the key with the correct shape is inserted and all the pins line up, the plug turns.
### Master Keys

![[Pasted image 20260527104500.png]]
In buildings with many doors, there will often be a **master key** that can be used to unlock any of them. To accomplish this, a **master pin** is placed in one or more shafts in each lock, making it so that the pins can meet at two possible levels to allow the plug to turn.
