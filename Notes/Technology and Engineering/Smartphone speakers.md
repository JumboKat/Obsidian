Smartphone speakers can be very loud despite their extremely small size.

![[Pasted image 20260529101035.png]]
Most smartphones have two speakers; one at the top for phone calls, and a larger, louder, general **loudspeaker**. While the larger of the two, it only weighs about as much as a dime (2.272g) and is about the same in size, with the smaller speaker weighs only a fifth (0.446g) of its counterpart's weight. Both speakers have the same basic components and functions.
### Components of a Speaker
![[Pasted image 20260529101345.png]]Within each speaker are four integral components: the diaphragm, voice coil, magnet, and the spider. Additionally, there is a **plastic case**, two **pole plates**, and a **front cover** provide uniformity for the magnetic field, structural support, and protection.
#### The Diaphragm
![[Pasted image 20260529101525.png]]
The **diaphragm** is a thin plastic sheet that moves back and forth to generate sound waves. When the diaphragm moves forward, it compresses air into high-pressure waves, then moves back to produce low-pressure waves. It can do this over a thousand times per second, generating an oscillation between high- and low-pressure waves. These waves are emitted from the speaker and are perceived as sound by our ears.
#### The Voice Coil and Magnet
![[Pasted image 20260529102009.png]]
The **voice coil** is a coil of insulated copper wire attached firmly to the diaphragm. 

![[Pasted image 20260529102109.png]]
The voice coil encloses a permanent **magnet** made of neodymium.

![[Pasted image 20260529102302.png]]
When a current runs through the coil, a temporary magnetic field is induced around it, which interacts with the neodymium magnet's magnetic field. Depending on the amount of current sent through the coil, the strength and size of its temporary magnetic field changes. Running a fluctuating current induces the diaphragm and coil to move forward and backward as it is attracted and repelled by the permanent magnet.
#### The Spider
![[Pasted image 20260529102608.png]]
The **spider** is a thin, clear, flexible, flat ring of plastic that prevents side-to-side movement in the diaphragm.

![[Pasted image 20260529102710.png]]
The spider acts like a trampoline, only allowing movement forward and backward. It applies counteracting tension to the direction of motion (when the diaphragm moves too far in one direction, the spider pulls it back with proportional force) and returns it to its central position. This limits the horizontal range of the diaphragm and preventing it from hitting the magnet and causing damage.
