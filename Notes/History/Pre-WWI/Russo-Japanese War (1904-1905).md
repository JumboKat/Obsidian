Tags: #Russia #Japan 

The Russo-Japanese War was fought between the Russian Empire and the Empire of Japan over imperial influence in Manchuria and Korea. The war ended in Russia's defeat after the Battle of Tsushima and the signing of the Treaty of Portsmouth, furthering the Russian Empire's decline and solidifying Japan's position as a world power on the international stage.
### Prelude
During the late 19th century, under the [[Meiji Restoration]], Japan began adopting Western sciences, technologies, and policies, fast-tracking its own modernization and making itself the strongest power in East Asia, having defeated its great rival China in the [[First Sino-Japanese War (1894-1895)|First Sino-Japanese War]].

After its defeat in the [[Crimean War (1853-1856)]], the Russian Empire began expanding its interests in the East. Between 1860 and 1901, Russia made a series of moves to increase its influence in the region, including the following: founding the port city of Vladivostok, meaning "Lord of the East" on ceded Chinese territory in 1860, wresting control over the strategic naval base at Port Arthur from the Japanese, and occupying Manchuria following the [[The Boxer Rebellion (1899-1901)|The Boxer Rebellion]]. Japan viewed Russian expansion as a threat to it and its influence over Korea. In response, Japan and Great Britain formed an alliance in 1902, designed to protect each other's interests in Asia. Both nations agreed to maintain neutrality if one was at war with Russia, or support each other if another power were to get involved.

![[Pasted image 20260411115500.png]]

After negotiations failed in 1904, Japan began preparing for war. At this time, 82% of the nation's budget went to the military, which was about 400,000 strong. The army made a point of dissolving old clan loyalties in favour of Japanese nationalism. Its navy was modelled after the British Royal Navy, with modern British-made capital ships. In 1904, it boasted the 4th largest navy with 6 battleships and 6 battle cruisers. Japan also coordinated with Great Britain to maintain an intelligence network.

On the other hand, Russia's military was severely outdated and poorly maintained. While it boasted an army of 1 million soldiers, only 150,000 of them were situated in the far East. The army was mainly comprised of poorly-trained conscripts with little motivation. Officers were chosen from the aristocracy, an archaic practice at the time, and non-noble officers who earned their position through promotion were rare. The navy was also just as lacking; many sailors came from landlocked provinces with little maritime experience, and while Russia maintained a larger navy than the Japanese in the region (7 battleships and 11 cruisers), their warships were outdated in comparison.

The Russian government did not plan or prepare for war with Japan, finding comfort in their believed racial superiority as a European power. They believed that a war would only start if they decided so. Meanwhile, Japan planned on a surprise attack on the Russian fleet at **Port Arthur**, believing that securing their supply lines would be vital to winning the conflict to come. 
### Attack on Port Arthur
Japan formally declared war on Russia on February 8th, 1904. However, before the declaration reached the Tsar in Moscow, the Japanese launched a night attack on the fleet in port. The attack was a complete surprise to the Russians, who were not expecting an attack and were not fully alert. While material damage on both sides was not significant, it achieved the strategic goal of scaring the fleet into staying in port, giving the Japanese the freedom to land troops in Korea, as they had already been doing at the international port of **Chemulpo**. They also destroyed the Russian ships docked there, who put up a fight despite the odds stacked against them.

The Japanese began a blockade of Port Arthur in April of 1904. **Russian Admiral Stepan Makarov** led aggressive naval sorties against the blockading fleet. This was put to an end, however, when the flag ship **Petropavlovsk** struck a mine and sank with all hands, including the admiral. Japanese troops also moved from Dalniy and besieged Port Arthur from the landward side. Though they were able to easily capture the town within a day during the [[First Sino-Japanese War (1894-1895)]], in 1904 the Russian defenders were much more well-defended and equipped, inflicting heavy losses on the first, suicidal assaults by the Japanese, the scale of which was hidden from the public by the Japanese government.
### Battle of Yalu River
![[Pasted image 20260521105155.png]]
42,000 troops of the Japanese First Army landed in Chemulpo, and more were planned to land at **Dalniy** in order to cut off Port Arthur. To secure this second landing, the First Army would have to push through 19,000 of the Russian Eastern detachment—including Russian Cossacks—along the **Yalu River**, which served as a natural defensive position. While Russian command was split on whether to retreat or stay, **General Mikhail Zasulich** ultimately chose to dig in.

The Japanese were much more covert in their approach, digging pits to hide their artillery guns and disguising as Korean fishermen when carrying out reconnaissance. The Russians made no effort to conceal their movements nor disguise their large artillery carriages. When Japanese engineers built a bridge in plain view of the Russian artillery, the Russians hastily opened fire and exposed their position, which in turn brought their destruction by the concealed guns of the Japanese.

![[Pasted image 20260521110624.png]]
Zasulich expected the Japanese to attack near the wide, shallow mouth of the river where they could rely on their naval guns for support. Instead, the 12th division crossed the river some distance from the Russian flank. Thinking this was just a small diversionary force, Zasulich did not respond and held the Russian position, allowing the 12th division to cross the Ai River and take the high ground. The Russian left flank subsequently collapsed. On May 1st, the Japanese followed up with a general assault across the Yalu and forced the Russians to retreat. As the 12th division began to encircle their position, many were forced to surrender.

The victory at the Yalu River paved the way for Japan's landings in Dalniy and the attack on Port Arthur via land. The Russian defeat by the Japanese removed any notions of the war being short and the Japanese being an easy enemy. The war seemed to be in Japan's favour, and many foreign banks now began to loan much-needed cash.
### Battle of the Yellow Sea
With the death of Admiral Stepan Makarov, **Admiral Wilgelm Vitgeft** was made commander of the fleet and was ordered to make an escape with his fleet from Port Arthur to join up with the cruisers stationed at Vladivostok, an order which he reluctantly obeyed. The Russian pacific fleet was comprised of 6 battleships, 4 cruisers, and 8 destroyers, while the Japanese awaited them in the **Yellow Sea** with 4 battleships, 10 cruisers, and 18 torpedo boat destroyers.

As the Japanese chased the Russians, both sides exchanged heavy fire. Togo's flagship **Mikasa** was hit and forced to withdraw. Just as the Russians seemed to be slipping away, Vitgeft's flagship **Tsesarevich** was struck at the bridge, killing him. The ship jammed in a port-ward turn, stirring confusion among the Russian fleet, but they and the Tsesarevich were saved by the battleship **Retvizan** who made a daring charge against the Japanese. The Russian fleet limped back to Port Arthur and awaited reinforcements: the **Baltic Fleet**, which was renamed the **Second Pacific Fleet** and set off in October 1904 in an 8 month-long trip around the world.

Throughout the summer of 1904, the Japanese gradually forced the Russian land forces to retreat to the Manchurian town of **Mukden**, and in January 1905, the Russians in Port Arthur capitulated, allowing the Japanese artillery to sink the Russian Pacific Fleet in the harbour. Although Japan had enjoyed a series of strategic victories, its casualties were mounting. Japan knew it needed to end the war quickly, else they be ground down by Russia's vast troop reserves, reinforcements which arrived via the **Trans-Siberian Railway**. 
### Battle of Mukden
The Japanese envisioned a large, decisive battle to end the war short. After the fall of Port Arthur, the Japanese were able to coalesce its five armies as one force. Japanese Commander **Oyama Iwao** predicted that the Russians were planning a counterattack, and so decided to strike first at Mukden. Though the Japanese were vastly outnumbered in men, cavalry, and artillery, they boasted twice as many machine guns as did the Russians. 

![[Pasted image 20260521184001.png]]
Like at the Yalu River, Oyama planned to outflank the Russian position in a pincer movement to prevent their escape. On February 17th, the Japanese Yalu Army moved onto the Russian Eastern flank in coordination with an artillery barrage that pinned the Russian centre. Believing the main attack was in the East, the Russians shifted accordingly and left the western flank weakened, which is where the Japanese main attack began. The third army swing around to try and cut-off the Russian retreat. Chaos erupted as the Russians panicked. Despite this, a rearguard was put together to prevent the Russian's total destruction. The Japanese were too exhausted at this point to pursue the Russians, who were thus mostly able to escape, and sustained a heavy loss of 25%. The battle of Mukden was likely the largest in scale up to this point in history. The Japanese had failed to achieve a decisive victory, and their land forces were burnt out. If the war were to end, it would have to be won at sea.
### Battle of Tsushima
![[Pasted image 20260521184730.png]]
After completing its 29,000 km voyage around the world, the Russian Baltic fleet was in poor condition. Throughout its journey it faced mutinies, and refueling issues, and mechanical problems. The fleet's commander Admiral **Zinovy Rozhestvensky** even referred to its oldest ships as "self sinkers." The Japanese were well aware of the fleet's journey and used the time to make repairs and preparations in expectation of their arrival. Admiral **Togo Heihachiro** planned to intercept the Baltic fleet at the 50 km-wide **Tsushima Strait** on their way to Vladivostok. Torpedo boats would ambush them at night, followed by the main fleet the next day.

![[Pasted image 20260522110700.png]]
The Japanese sighted the Russian fleet early on May 27th, but conditions at sea were too rough for torpedo boats, forcing an all-out attack by the Japanese main fleet. That afternoon, the Japanese fleet crossed in front of the Russians and performed a u-turn on its portside, then travelled obliquely towards them and opened fire, with the Mikasa badly hit in the return fire. While the Russians had heavier guns, the Japanese had a better rate of fire and better fire control. As the Japanese crossed the Russian T, the angle of attack meant the Russians could only use their forward-facing turrets, while the Japanese could fire all available guns broadsides. Within the first hour, Rozhestvensky was wounded after the Russian flagship was hit. The battleship **Oslyabya** was sunk soon afterward, the first modern ship to be sunk by gunnery alone, followed by the **Knyaz Suvorov**. The battleship **Aleksandr III** charged the Japanese line in an all-out assault, giving the Russians some reprieve. When the battle resumed at 7pm, however, the Russian ships were damaged and weak, and soon the Aleksandr III and the **Borodino** were sunk.

During the night, the Russians were harried by destroyers and torpedo boats. By the next morning, only two battleships remained, and the Japanese had surrounded what remained of the Baltic fleet, forcing their surrender. The Russians had suffered a complete defeat at Tsushima; 34 of 38 ships having been sunk, captured, or interned in neutral ports, including all of the battleships, with 5000 sailors killed. The Japanese suffered minor losses, losing three torpedo boats and 110 dead. With the Russian navy now essentially non-existent, **Tsar Nicolas II** agreed to negotiate the terms of peace.
### Treaty of Portsmouth
The peace talks were mediated by the United States in New Hampshire, with President **Theodore Roosevelt** heavily involved. Both Russia and Japan needed the war to end quickly. While Japan was in a winning position, they were militarily exhausted, and Russia was pressured by political unrest at home. Japanese troops occupied **Sakhalin** island in July to pressure the Russians, who in turn sent reinforcements to Manchuria. The terms of the **Treaty of Portsmouth** stated that Russia were to do as follows:
- Withdraw from Manchuria and the Liaodong Peninsula
- Grant most of the railway concessions to Japan
- Allow Japan to retain control over South Sakhalin
- Recognize Japanese dominance over nominally independent Korea
- Not pay a war indemnity to Japan, who were now bankrupt
### Aftermath
President Theodore Roosevelt was awarded the Nobel Peace Price for his involvement. The Japanese public, unaware of Japan's tenuous position, viewed the terms too lenient, leading to riots and martial law. While the terms were not as controversial in Russia, it fueled the flames of the 1905 Revolution, an event which the autocracy had narrowly managed to survive. Russia was greatly weakened, while Japan solidified its position as a major power.

The Russo-Japanese War had a profound impact on military thinking at the time. It served as a demonstration for the destructive effectiveness of modern weaponry, like the machine gun. Many would draw the conclusion that modern warfare could be won quickly and decisively.
