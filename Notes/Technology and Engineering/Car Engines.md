The engine is the source of life in a car. It converts the heat from the combustion of an air-fuel mixture to the forces that power the car and turn its wheels.
### Engine Components

![[Pasted image 20260529105010.png|316]]![[Pasted image 20260529103604.png|349]]
The **engine block** is the main support structure and is cast as a single unit from iron and aluminum alloys; it holds the crankshaft and cylinders. At the heavy bottom of the engine is the **cylinder block**, which houses the main moving parts of the engine.

![[Pasted image 20260529154426.png]]
The cylinder block supports the pistons, crankshaft, connecting rods, and the oil pan. The upper **cylinder head**, which is detachable, supports the **camshaft**, **cam lobe**, **intake/exhaust valves** that open and close as fuel enters the combustion chamber.

![[Pasted image 20260529105210.png|132]]![[Pasted image 20260529105420.png|242]]![[Pasted image 20260529105947.png|133]]![[Pasted image 20260529105621.png|155]]
**Pistons** are mostly made by casting or forging cast iron or aluminum alloys, making them able to withstand the high temperatures and pressures associated with combustion. The explosive force of the air-fuel mixture drives the upward motion of the pistons, which turn the **crankshaft**. The torque of the crankshaft rotates the wheels of the car. The crankshaft has counterweights to balance against the pistons to ensure smooth revolutions. The **connecting rods** connect the pistons to the crank shaft, and convert their reciprocal vertical motion into the crankshaft's rotational motion. 

The **combustion chamber** is the area within each **cylinder**. The highest position the piston reaches inside the cylinder is the **top dead centre** (TDC), while the lowest position is the **bottom dead centre (BDC)**. A **stroke** occurs when the piston travels the entire way in one direction. Over the course of one compression stroke, the piston travels from the BDC to the TDC, increasing the pressure and temperature of the air-fuel mixture. Once the piston reaches the TDC, the **spark plug** ignites the fuel. Car engines operate on a 4 stroke cycle.
### The 4 Stroke Cycle

![[Pasted image 20260529190316.png]]
 In the first stroke (**intake**), the piston descends, creating a low-pressure vacuum that sucks in air-fuel mixture through the **intake port**, with both **intake valves** open.

![[Pasted image 20260529190600.png]]
In the second stroke (**compression**), both valves are closed, and the piston pushes upward, compressing the air-fuel mixture for a more powerful combustion.

![[Pasted image 20260529190713.png]]
In the third stroke (the **power stroke**), the spark plug delivers an electrical spark, igniting the air-fuel mixture and pushing the piston down via explosive force. A **connecting rod** transfers the energy to the crankshaft.

![[Pasted image 20260529190927.png]]
In the fourth stroke (**exhaust**), the piston rises again, pushing the spent mixture out the exhaust valves and the exhaust port.]

For a smooth, continuous delivery of power, pistons take turns firing. Some engines, like the **V6** and **V8**, can have more pistons, each connected in pairs in the shape of a V.

![[Pasted image 20260613102728.png]]
The **camshaft** is a metal rod that contains several **cams**, which are egg-shaped steel plates. The camshaft controls the timing of the intake and exhaust valves, with cams pushing spring-loaded valves in turn.

![[Pasted image 20260613102854.png]]
The **timing belt** synchronizes the rotation of both the crankshaft and the **camshaft** to ensure the engine valves open and close at the right time. It is composed of a heavy-duty rubber band and cogs to grasp the pulleys of the crankshaft and camshaft. 


![[Pasted image 20260529110040.png|180]]![[Pasted image 20260529110650.png|216]]![[Pasted image 20260529110804.png|280]]
The **oil pan** sits at the bottom of the engine, just below the crankshaft, to collect lubricating oil. All moving parts of the engine are lubricated to reduce friction. The **flywheel** is mounted on the crankshaft to balance the vehicle's speed, as the speed and torque of different parts of the car's engine are not uniform. It is also where the **starter** connects to the system.
### Air Intake
![[Pasted image 20260613104615.png]]
Air enters the system via the grille at the car's front. It passes through an air filter, then into the **intake manifold**. Here it is mixed with fuel and then sucked into the individual cylinders through the intake ports.
### Fuel
![[Pasted image 20260613104853.png]]
The **fuel pump** carries gas from the tank (located at the rear of the car) through fuel lines, passing through a **fuel filter**, then to the engine.

![[Pasted image 20260613105033.png]]
At the engine, the **fuel injectors** spray fuel into the intake port at precisely-timed intervals, in-sync with the rest of the system's timing.
### Cooling
Because of the combustion of the air-fuel mixture, the engine heats up during operation.
