### The Zulu Kingdom
![[Pasted image 20260606233926.png]]
In the 1870s, three main powers compete for territory in Southern Africa: Great Britain, the Boer Republics, and the Zulu Kingdom. The Zulu Kingdom came to be when King **Shaka Zulu** spearheaded a revolution in 1816, uniting the various clans under the **AmaZulu** (or Zulu). 

![[Pasted image 20260606234712.png]]
As ruler of the new kingdom, Shaka introduced many reforms in the military. He introduced conscription for all able-bodied men, with training starting at the age of 14. The **impis** (warriors) are equipped with short stabbing **Assegai** (spears) called **iKlwa**, named after the sound it makes when ripped from an enemy's flesh. Along with an assegai, warriors wielded a cowhide shield (**isiHlangu**). Other weapons include the **isiJula** (throwing spear) and the **iWisa** (club).

![[Pasted image 20260606234842.png]]
Additionally, Shaka introduces a successful battle formation called **izimpondo zankomo** (the horns of the buffalo), which is comprised of four sections of soldiers: the chest to engage the enemy and hold them in place, the two horns to outflank and encircle the enemy, and the loins serving as the reserve. With these military advancements, Shaka transforms tribal warfare from what was merely a ritual show of force into campaigns of crushing annihilation and annexation. Through this, conquered tribes are incorporated into the kingdom, and his army grows from 350 to about 20,000 men in just a few years, with his kingdom as large as 30,000 square km. Though Shaka is assassinated by his two half-brothers in 1828, his military empire lives on.
### European Arrival
In 1824, the British establish what eventually becomes the trading hub of **Durban**. Tensions rise between the Zulu and the British as the latter seek to expand their territory. In the 1830s, the Boers/Voortrekkers, who had been living in Africa since the 17th century, were now migrating westward to establish new republics as tensions between them and the British increase. These new Boer territories conflict with Zulu territory, which eventually leads to border clashes
### Battle of Blood River
![[Pasted image 20260607001014.png]]
In 1838, during the Boers' **Great Trek**, the Boers and the Zulu agreed to a peaceable land treaty, however this was broken by the Zulu King, believing he had the strength in numbers to overpower the Boers. During the **Piet Retief Delegation massacre** in February 1838, after the treaty between the two peoples had just been negotiated, the Zulus massacred 100 Boers of the delegation party, starting a wider war between the Boers, British, and the Zulus, who were undergoing an internal power struggle at the time. The Zulu warriors sack the porty city of Durban, but are dealt a devastating defeat at **Blood River**, with over 3,000 Zulu casualties to just three lost by the Boers. In the aftermath of the war, the Zulus are forced to cede land to the Boers and the British.

Skirmishes between the powers continue on in the decades following. In the 1870s, the British begin pursuing a policy to consolidate their power and bring the Zulus and Boers under their rule, believing that it is their duty to 'westernize' the natives. in 1877, the British annex the Transvaal republic, who at this point is bankrupt after constant fighting, and who fears Zulu invasion.
### Ultimatum to Cetshwayo
![[Pasted image 20260609140758.png|361]]
The British now seek to incorporate the Zulu territories, but King **Cetshwayo** refuses, upset about the constant Boer incursions into his territory. The government in London seeks to avoid war and strictly forbids colonial authorities from starting one, but their orders are defied. On December 11, 1878, they present to Cetshwayo an ultimatum with terms made harsh for the purpose of being rejected. They demand that the Zulu:
- pay fines for percieved offences
- turn over several chiefs to the British
- disband the army
- end the Zulu military
- give more rights to Christians
- surrender all political power to a British Agent
The King rejects the ultimatum as expected, providing the casus belli for a British invasion of the Zulu Kingdom, which starts on January 11, 1879. Leading the invasion is **Lord Chelmsford**, who had previous experience fighting in the [[Crimean War (1853-1856)||Crimean War]] and colonial conflicts, such as the war against the **Xhosa** people, and had come to form a low opinion of African warriors in general.

![[Pasted image 20260609142234.png]]
The British begin the war with a force of about 7800 men, armed with the breech-loading **Martini-Henry** rifle, with the support of several cannons, a rocket battery, and some gatling guns. Alongside them is the **Natal Native Contingent**, with native African troops serving under a white British officers. Only 1 in 10 are allowed a firearm, that being the outdated **Snider** rifle; the rest are armed with assegai. With poor training, they play more of a supporting role, and to distinguish themselves from the enemy Zulus, they wear red bandanas. Another supporting force is the **Natal Native Horse***, comprised mainly of those from the **amaNgwane** tribes who are longstanding enemies of the Zulu. They wear tan uniforms, ride basuto ponies and are armed with carbines alongside their assegai. The last component of the British invasion are white settler soldiers like the **Natal Carbineers**. Meeting the British in battle are 35,000 warriors under King Cetshwayo.
### First British Invasion
![[Pasted image 20260609144121.png]]
Lord Chelmsford's plan is to have three columns march on the Zulu capital of **Ulundi**, with the left- and right-most columns drawing troops away to pave the way for Chelmsford's central column to march straight to Ulundi and engage the main Zulu force. His plan encounters complications when his advance is slowed by heavy rains that muddies the roads and swells rivers, compounding on the issue of pulling the wagon supply trains. Believing that the Zulu army won't reach him until they are close to the capital, Chelmsford sends out scouting sorties ahead of the main column. Cetshwayo's army moves much faster than the British expect, however, and locate the main column, drawing the scouting parties away to weaken it. Even Chelmsford himself being led away from the main British camp at **Isandlwana**, forgoing British military guidelines by leaving without fortifying the camp. 
#### Battle of Isandlwana
The British, upon seeing the Zulu scouts, believe they are just isolated units. In fact, an army of 20,000 men are bearing down on the camp. When the Zulus came within range of the camp, their leaders were initially hesitant, but were reassured by their scouts that the British were scattered and weak, and thus, commenced the attack. Meanwhile, Chelmsford is 20km away enjoying a picnic, and acting commander Lt-Colonel **Henry Pulleine** is informed of the Zulu presence, but underestimates their size, and chooses not to fortify the camp. Only 1800 men and 2 cannons are present at the camp.

![[Pasted image 20260611193658.png]]
The Zulus make a stealthy approach toward the camp, but at 11:00am they are spotted by chance by riders chasing loose cattle, so they decide to launch an immediate full-scale attack. Using the 'horns of the buffalo' formation, the main 'chest' unit descend from the valley in a wide sweeping front, while the 'horns' attack the flanks in a pincer movement.

![[Pasted image 20260611193902.png]]
Instead of retreating to a more defensible position at the base of Isandlwana hill, Pulleine decides to establish a thin perimeter at the edge of the camp. While the intense rifle-fire of the British are able to halt the chest's advance, however the Zulu 'horns' hone in on their flanks. Running out of ammunition, the men pull back towards the core of the camp to be closer to their ammo supplies, but as they do so, the Zulu take the opportunity to charge them with full force. The battle soon turns into a massacre. Some mounted units are able to make their way out, leaving infantry units to fend for themselves. The Zulu take no prisoners, and 1300 British soldiers are killed. With the camp at Isandlwana, the British central invasion column is destroyed.
#### Battle of Rorke's Drift
On the border river between Natal and the Zulu Kingdom is a mission station known as **Rorke's Drift** that is being used as a hospital and supply depot under the command of Lieutenant **John Chard**. In the noon of January 22, two survivors arrive at the station warning of the disaster at Isandlwana and the imminent attack on Rorke's Drift. Knowing they won't be able to outrun the Zulu, Chard decides to stay and fight, hastily erecting a defensive perimeter to be defended by about 400 men. Approaching them 4000 Zulu warriors under the command of Prince **Dabulamanzi kaMpande**, the half-brother of Cetshwayo. Having been the reserve force at Isandlwana, the prince now seeks to win his share of the glory, and disobeys the King's orders not to cross the river into British territory. On their arrival, both the Natal Native Horse and Natal Native Contingent desert the station, leaving just 150 men at its defense, forcing Chard to fall back. As brutal hand-to-hand fighting ensues, they are forced to retreat even more, leaving the hospital—and all of its patients—to the Zulu.

As night falls, the remaining British make a last stand, with the flames of the burning hospital illuminating the background around them. However, the Zulus become uncoordinated in the darkness, and the British are able to repel each new attack; the Zulu assault gradually weakens, before grounding to a halt at 2:00 am, though shots are still exchanged until 4:00. After 12 hours, the battle was over, with up to 850 Zulu casualties and 14 British dead, though the majority are wounded in some way.
#### Battle of Kambula
With the central column now disintegrated, the Zulu begin to bear down on the right column under Colonel **Charles Pearson**. To create a diversion, Colonel **Evelyn Wood** takes the left column and attacks the Zulu hill fort at **Hlobane** on January 28th. Here, the Zulu hold their ground from within their position while pelting the British using captured rifles. The British attack is uncoordinated thanks in part to a thunderstorm, and one third are killed. Afterward, they retreat to a defensive position at **Kambula**.

The Zulu success at Hlobane makes their commander **Mnyamana kaNgqengelele** overconfident, who proceeds to disobey Cetshwayo's orders not to attack any British fortified postions. He leads 20,000 warriors, many of whom previously fought at Isandlwana and are armed with captured rifles, to surround and attack the British at Kambula. But Wood has learned from the British defeats, and creates a **wagon laager** (or wagon fort; a circle of wagons forming a defensive encampment) and clear the ground around them to create a killing field for rifle fire and their six 7-pounder cannons. The Zulus attack and regroup several times, but each time they are cut down by the British rifles and cannons. Eventually, they retreat, which turns into a rout as the 600 British cavalry are sent after them. With about 80 British casualties compared to 2000 Zulu, the battle is a decisive British victory.
