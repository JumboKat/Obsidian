
The Russo-Japanese War was fought between the Russian Empire and the Empire of Japan over imperial influence in Manchuria and Korea. The war ended in Russia's defeat after the Battle of Tsushima and the signing of the Treaty of Portsmouth, furthering the Russian Empire's decline and solidifying Japan's position as a world power on the international stage.
### Prelude
During the late 19th century, under the [[Meiji Restoration]], Japan began adopting Western sciences, technologies, and policies, fast-tracking its own modernization and making itself the strongest power in East Asia, having defeated its great rival China in the [[First Sino-Japanese War (1894-1895)]].

After Russia's defeat in the [[Crimean War (1853-1856)]], the Russian Empire began expanding its interests in the East. Between 1860 and 1901, Russia made a series of moves to increase its influence in the region, including the following: founding the port city of Vladivostok, meaning "Lord of the East" on ceded Chinese territory in 1860, wresting control over the strategic naval base at Port Arthur from the Japanese, and occupying Manchuria following the [[The Boxer Rebellion (1899-1901)]]. Japan viewed Russian expansion as a threat to it and its influence over Korea. In response, Japan and Great Britain formed an alliance in 1902, designed to protect each other's interests in Asia. Both nations agreed to maintain neutrality if one was at war with Russia, or support each other if another power were to get involved.

After negotiations failed in 1904, Japan began preparing for war. At this time, 82% of the nation's budget went to the military, which was about 400,000 strong. The army made a point of dissolving old clan loyalties in favour of Japanese nationalism. Its navy was modelled after the British Royal Navy, with modern British-made capital ships. In 1904, it boasted the 4th largest navy with 6 battleships and 6 battle cruisers. Japan also coordinated with Great Britain to maintain an intelligence network.

On the other hand, Russia's military was severely outdated and poorly maintained. While it boasted an army of 1 million soldiers, only 150,000 of them were situated in the far East. The army was mainly comprised of poorly-trained conscripts with little motivation. Officers were chosen from the aristocracy, an archaic practice at the time, and non-noble officers who earned their position through promotion were rare. The navy was also just as lacking; many sailors came from landlocked provinces with little maritime experience, and while Russia maintained a larger navy than the Japanese in the region (7 battleships and 11 cruisers), their warships were outdated in comparison.
