The engine is the source of life in a car. It converts the heat from the combustion of an air-fuel mixture to the forces that power the car and turn its wheels.
### Engine Components

![[Pasted image 20260529105010.png|316]]![[Pasted image 20260529103604.png|349]]
The **engine block** is the main support structure and is cast as a single unit from iron and aluminum alloys. At the heavy bottom of the engine is the **cylinder block**, which houses the main moving parts of the engine.

![[Pasted image 20260529103216.png]]
The cylinder block supports the pistons, crankshaft, connecting rods, and the oil pan. The upper cylinder head, which is detachable, supports the **camshaft**, **cam lobe**, **intake/exhaust valves** that open and close as fuel enters the combustion chamber.

![[Pasted image 20260529105210.png|132]]![[Pasted image 20260529105420.png|242]]![[Pasted image 20260529105947.png|133]]![[Pasted image 20260529105621.png|155]]
**Pistons** are mostly made by casting or forging cast iron or aluminum alloys, making them able to withstand the high temperatures and pressures associated with combustion. The explosive force of the air-fuel mixture drives the upward motion of the pistons, which turn the **crankshaft**. The torque of the crankshaft rotates the wheels of the car. The **connecting rods** connect the pistons to the crank shaft, and convert their reciprocal vertical motion into the crankshaft's rotational motion. 

The **combustion chamber** is the area within each **cylinder**. The highest position the piston reaches inside the cylinder is the **top dead centre** (TDC), while the lowest position is the **bottom dead centre (BDC)**. Over the course of one compression stroke, the piston travels from the BDC to the TDC, increasing the pressure and temperature of the air-fuel mixture. Once the piston reaches the TDC, the **spark plug** ignites the fuel.

![[Pasted image 20260529110040.png|180]]![[Pasted image 20260529110650.png|216]]![[Pasted image 20260529110804.png|280]]
The **timing belt** synchronizes the rotation of both the crankshaft and the **camshaft** to ensure the engine valves open and close at the right time. It is composed of a heavy-duty rubber band and cogs to grasp the pulleys of the crankshaft and camshaft. The **oil pan** sits at the bottom of the engine, just below the crankshaft, to collect lubricating oil. All moving parts of the engine are lubricated to reduce friction. The **flywheel** is mounted on the crankshaft to balance the vehicle's speed, as the speed and torque of different parts of the car's engine are not uniform.
