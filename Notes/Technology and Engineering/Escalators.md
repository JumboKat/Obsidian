### History of the Escalator
![[Pasted image 20260513100533.png|226]]![[Pasted image 20260513100608.png|427]]
The idea of the escalator started out as an 1859 patent called "Revolving Stairs," but it wasn't until the "Inclined Elevator" in 1896 when the first actual escalator was in operation.
### Use of Escalators
Nowadays, escalators are widespread, mainly used in places with large quantities of people to move, such as shopping malls, airports, and subway stations. It can move much more people at a time compared to elevators, though an elevator is usually found nearby for those who cannot use escalators. 

![[Pasted image 20260513101635.png|296]]![[Pasted image 20260513101710.png|364]]
Variants include the moving sidewalk (often found in airports) and the curved escalator.
### How an escalator works
![[Pasted image 20260513101920.png]]
The moving stairs are comprised of individual steps. 
![[Pasted image 20260515013140.png]]
The top surface is ridged to facilitate grip. They can also be made of rubber. The edges are painted yellow, warning passengers not to stand too close to the edge. At the top and bottom where passengers board, the steps form a flat surface. Two wheels at the bottom of each step guide its movement.

![[Pasted image 20260515013429.png]]
Step chains attach to the top of either side of the step.

![[Pasted image 20260515013706.png]]
The step chain and the bottom wheels move along a track, which is positioned so that the steps remain level as they descend or ascend. At the end, the steps flip over and return to the beginning via the underside of the track.

![[Pasted image 20260515013945.png|445]]The motor is typically located at the top of the escalator, and must move an axle connected to two gears that pull the step chain. They are connected via gears and a roller chain.

![[Pasted image 20260515014903.png|337]]![[Pasted image 20260515014938.png|336]]
Axle and gear system connects to the first by a roller chain, which moves the handrail. The movement of the handrail and the steps are synced. The whole system is encased in a strong metal truss. 

![[Pasted image 20260515015214.png]]At the top and bottom of the escalator lies the cone plate. The steps go just underneath the cone plate; their ridges also interlock, minimizing the chances of something getting caught underneath.
