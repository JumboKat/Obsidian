### Prelude
In the early 1890s, Korea finds itself the centre of attention of larger powers, those being China, Japan, Russia, and Great Britain. During this time, the country is plagued by political instability as well, with great debate on whether to modernize and embrace Western technologies similar to Japan during the Meiji restoration, or increase its dependency on Qing China. 

By 1894, **King Kojong** is left as head of a weak and corrupt government following a series of coups and uprisings. The Donghak movement, which combines the ideas of Buddhism, Confucianism, and Taoism, oppose the dynasty's 'moral degeneracy' and western influences. The movement gains traction as more followers join their cause. A protest breaks out, which the local government troops are insufficient in dealing with, forcing the king to plead the Qing for help. The Chinese send a small force to Korea and informs Japan as per an 1885 agreement, but only after the troops had set sail. This is considered a breach of the agreement by Japan, causing it to send its own troops as well. 

**Yuan Shikai**, the Chinese representative in Korea, proposes a withdrawal from both sides, but Japanese Prime Minister **Hirobumi Ito** is put under pressure to be aggressive by ministers and the patriotic press. He doesn't want to appear too aggressive to the British, since they are about to end their unequal treaty with Japan, so Japan simply states that their troops will remain until Korea enacts reforms to deal with the causes of the rebellion.

China's top diplomat **Viceroy Li** tries to get the Russians and British to mediate for them, but they refuse. He is keen on avoiding conflict, doubting China's military readiness, and knowing that Empress Dowager Cixi had recently diverted funds to build a new palace for her upcoming birthday. The Emperor Guanxu considers war, wanting to overcome the influence of his aunt Empress Dowager Cixi, but is opposed by most of his ministers, though he at the very least reinforces the troops in Korea. 
### Declaration of War
At this point, Japan sees war as an inevitability, and demands King Kojong to expel the Chinese and adopt reforms to prove its independence from China. On Julay 25th, Japanese warships attack the Chinese navy at the Battle of P'ung-Do, sinking a Chinese cruiser and troop transport. China declares war on Japan on August 1st, 1894.

The war in Japan is mostly supported by the public, with the conflict framed as a way of advancing Asia out of the old world order, a view shared by many in the West harbouring anti-Chinese sentiment. While China was a much larger opponent with more resources at its disposal, its military command was decentralized, and its weapons and tactics were outdated. Japan's soldiers were better trained, equipped, and led, with officers having studied western battle tactics. In prior years, funds from the Imperial Court had been diverted to fund the Japanese army and navy. In June of 1984, they had even cracked Chinese military and diplomatic codes.
### Battle of Pyongyang
Before the war had formally been declared, Japanese troops were already on the move, defeating the Chinese at Seonghwan. The Chinese abandon southern Korea altogether and march 250km north to Pyongyang. The city was well-protected by a river, hills, and fortifications. Despite this, the Chinese are routed when the Japanese attack the city from all four sides with the support of gunboats on September 15, 1894. Fragmented Chinese command results in friendly-fire among Chinese soldiers. Pyongyang is taken in just two days, with minimal casualties suffered by the Japanese.

### Battle of the Yalu River
At this point, China's **Beiyang** **Fleet** maintained control of the Yellow Sea and access to Manchuria and the Korean west coast, but it chooses to remain idle in lieu of taking the fight to the Japanese. The Japanese fleet under **Admiral Sukeyuki** Ito discovers the Chinese by chance. As the Japanese fleet move in and switch to a diagonal formation, the Chinese second-in-command Commodore Liu spontaneously orders the flagship's main guns to fire, knocking Admiral Ding and his British advisor Vice-Admiral **William Tyler** unconscious. With their naval leadership out of commission, the Chinese fleet is picked off one-by-one by Japanese concentrated fire. The Japanese eventually break off when their capital ship is hit. Five Chinese ships were sunk, whilst the Japanese had only sustained damage to three.

### Battle of Lüshunkou
Following two successive victories, the Japanese move into Chinese territory, with one army under **General Yamagata** crossing the Yalu towards **Shenyang**, and another towards Port Arthur, which held significant strategic importance, serving as the only base where Chinese ships could be repaired. The Japanese quickly conquered the many protective forts along the way to the city, and soon had the city surrounded by both land and naval forces. The Chinese, refusing surrender, opted to counterattack, but they are outmatched in every way by the Japanese. The Chinese commanders flee under disguise, and Japanese troops enter the city on November 21st. Up until now, they had behaved respectfully toward their defeated opponents, but rumours of combatants hiding among civilians, as well as the fact that Chinese soldiers had hung the severed heads of Japanese soldiers before the city fell, lead to a three-day massacre carried out by the Japanese, both inside and outside the city, and including women and children.

The incident, which threatened to cast the Japanese as barbaric, prompted the Japanese to carry out a dissemination campaign to convince the American press that reports were blown out of proportion. This was done so that American Congress would approve a trade treaty that would facilitate more favourable terms for Japan.
### Donghak Peasant Revolt
While Japan had invaded Korea under the pretense of protecting Korean independence, in actuality it wanted Korea under its own influence. For this purpose, the Japanese put King Kojong's father, **Daewon-gun**, in power in July. He and his conservative cohorts oppose Japanese-sponsored reforms, however. He makes pleas to the Chinese and even the Russians for support, and draws up plans of assassinating pro-Japanese Korean adversaries. Upon discovery of this plot, the Japanese remove him from power, prompting Daewon-gun organize an uprising by the Donghak rebels and local peasants, who, although were somewhat divided ideologically, were convinced to join the movement following continual Japanese repression and abuses by Korean officials.

What ensues is a messy, brutal civil war involving the Donghaks, Japanese troops, government forces, and local militias known as **minpos**. The fighting is ugly, involving beheadings, mutilation of corpses, and scorched-earth policy. Japanese reinforcements soon arrive, and they, along with Korean government forces, defeat the Donghaks in a series of battles throughout November and December. While smaller in size, they were much better equipped than the Donghaks, who lacked consolidation and were armed with matchlock muskets and bamboo spears. The Japanese spared few.

During this, the Qing face political turmoil in China, and attempt to negotiate peace. Suspecting that the Qing are stalling in the hopes of Western intervention, the Japanese decline and press on with their offensive. **General Yamagata** continues his advance on Shenyang, taking Haicheng in December. Here, the Chinese launch several counteroffensives in attempt to retake the city. Though they ultimately fail, they inflict enough casualties on the invaders to halt their continued advance.
### Battle of Weihaiwei
Although Japan's naval superiority remains uncontested, the surviving remnants of the Qing **Beiyang Fleet** are still at large, and present the threat of an enemy landing at Beijing, so the Japanese now turn to the Qing's last remaining naval base at **Weihaiwei**. A Japanese landing easily takes most of the outlying forts, guns, and the walled town of Weihaiwei, leaving only a naval headquarters on a nearby island. After dismissing an offer to defect from Admiral Ito, Chinese Admiral Ding attempts to retake one of the forts, an effort that ends in failure.

After receiving word that no reinforcements are coming, Ding negotiates for safe passage of his men, before committing suicide. 
### Treaty of Shimonoseki
Peace talks begin in April 1895 in Japan, but are interrupted for a week when Viceroy Li is shot in the face by a Japanese civilian, from which he recovers. On April 17th, 1895, the **Treaty of Shimonoseki** is signed. China is forced to recognize Korean autonomy, pay a large indemnity, open several major cities to Japan for trade, grant Japan the same preferential trade status as the Western Powers, and cede the Pescadores Islands, Liaodong Peninsula, including Port Arthur, and the island of Formosa (now called Taiwan).
### Aftermath
Following the Japanese takeover of Formosa, the island's residents began a doomed five-month insurrection against the Japanese occupation. Having suffered between 30,000 - 50,000 deaths, Korea left the war devastated. For the Qing, the loss had proven the failure and inadequacies of China's modernization efforts. 

The Japanese indulged in their victory, believing it proof of their place among the world powers, but this celebration was cut short by the **Triple Intervention** in May staged by Russia, France, and Germany, who argued that Japan's control over the Liaodong peninsula posed a threat to Korea, China, and Russia, with the latter even threatening military action against them. The Japanese reluctantly returned the territory, but in 1898, the Russians pressured the Chinese into leasing the Liaodong Peninsula and Port Arthur to them. Russia also had interfered with Japan's designs for Korean dominance. Japanese agents had assassinated Queen Min and restore Daewon-gun, only to see Russia help King Kojong regain power in 1896. The Russian roadblocks against Japanese expansion in Asia caused outrage among the Japanese. Tensions between the two powers would rise, until eventually exploding into the [[Russo-Japanese War (1904-1905)|Russo-Japanese War]]
