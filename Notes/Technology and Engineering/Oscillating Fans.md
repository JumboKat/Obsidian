![[Pasted image 20260516012841.png|276]]
The basic, common design of the oscillating fan includes the following:
- buttons at the bottom to adjust fan speed
- dial on the side to adjust pitch
- pin on top to toggle oscillation
### The Buttons
![[Pasted image 20260516013209.png|277]]![[Pasted image 20260516013425.png|393]]
There are four buttons to toggle between three different speeds, and on/off function. When one speed button is pushed, it stays down, while the others are released; this excludes the off button, which does not stay down when pressed. Underneath each button is a tensile spring that naturally pushes the button up. Underneath is a metal plate with jagged, downward cuts underneath each button. A spring pushes the plate to the right. Pushing down a button slides the plate to the left as the button slides along the sloped edge of the cut, until it eventually snags into place on the notch, preventing the button from springing back up. When another button is pressed, the plate slides enough to the left to dislodge the previous button. Since the purpose of the white button is to release all other buttons, it does not have a notch to hold it down. 

![[Pasted image 20260516013841.png]]
The buttons are connected to three colour-coded wires. Whichever button is pressed sends a signal through its respective wire up the fan and into the motor, which spins the fan at the specific speed.
### The Oscillation
#### The 4-bar Mechanism
To allow for cooling in a wider area, the fan can oscillate within an arc when the pin is pressed. To avoid using an additional motor for the oscillation, the rotation of the fan driven by the motor is converted into a side-to-side motion. The idea is based on the **4-bar mechanism**.

![[Pasted image 20260516014331.png]]
Four bars are attached to each other by pins, which they can rotate about. The **fixed bar** is fixed in place, while the **crank** is driven by a motor. The **follower** and **coupler** are forced to move along with the crank. 

![[Pasted image 20260516014753.png|697]]
![[Pasted image 20260516014822.png|324]]![[Pasted image 20260516014841.png|366]]
In this configuration, the blue bar is fixed, while the yellow is the crank. This causes the green bar to sweep side-to-side in a fixed-length arc. 

![[Pasted image 20260516084913.png|431]]
The same configuration is implemented in the oscillating fan. Here, the fixed bar is in front, attaching to a long bar and the motor (serving as the other long bar), with the crank in the back that rotates to get the fan to oscillate. The crank is connected to the **gear box** that sits behind the motor, which is responsible for translating the rotation of the fan into the rotation of the crank.
#### The Gear Box 
![[Pasted image 20260516085510.png|392]]![[Pasted image 20260516085454.png|257]]
Inside the gear box, a long screw-like piece called the **worm** (blue) sticks out the back of the motor that rotates along with the fan. As it spins, it spins the **worm gear** (purple). The pin, which is teethed, passes through the worm gear and connects it to another gear below (green) that spins the crank.

![[Pasted image 20260516085824.png|407]]![[Pasted image 20260516085931.png|265]]
Near the top of the pin is a spring with two **ball bearings**, which stick out just enough to catch onto the teeth of the worm gear to spin with it.

As the motor rotates the fan, the worm spins, causing the worm gear to spin, which spins the pin, causing the second gear to spin, rotating the crank and moving the 4-arm mechanism with it, producing oscillation. This only happens when the pin is down; when the pin is up, the ball bearings no longer catch onto the worm gear and thus, the crank will not rotate.

![[Pasted image 20260516090257.png|348]]![[Pasted image 20260516090348.png|283]]
When the pin is pulled up, the tension from the spring would send the ball bearings flying outward in opposite directions. The gear box lid encloses around the pin to prevent the escape of the ball bearings. The outward pressure from the ball bearings pushing against the walls of the lid is what prevents the pin from falling back down when it is pulled up. When the pin is pushed back down, the spring forces the ball bearings back out and in contact with the worm gear.

The combination of gears in the gear box ensures that the rotation of the crank (and thus the oscillation of the fan) is much slower than the spinning of the fan blades.
