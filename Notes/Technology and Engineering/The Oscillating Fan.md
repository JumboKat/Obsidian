The basic, common design of the oscillating fan includes the following:
- buttons at the bottom to adjust fan speed
- dial on the side to adjust pitch
- pin on top to toggle oscillation
### The Buttons

![[Pasted image 20260516013209.png|277]]![[Pasted image 20260516013425.png|393]]
There are four buttons to toggle between three different speeds, and on/off function. When one speed button is pushed, it stays down, while the others are released; this excludes the off button, which does not stay down when pressed. Underneath each button is a tensile spring that naturally pushes the button up. Underneath is a metal plate with jagged, downward cuts underneath each button. A spring pushes the plate to the right. Pushing down a button slides the plate to the left as the button slides along the sloped edge of the cut, until it eventually snags into place on the notch, preventing the button from springing back up. When another button is pressed, the plate slides enough to the left to dislodge the previous button. Since the purpose of the white button is to release all other buttons, it does not have a notch to hold it down. 

![[Pasted image 20260516013841.png]]
The buttons are connected to three colour-coded wires. Whichever button is pressed sends a signal through its respective wire up the fan and into the motor, which spins the fan at the specific speed.

### The Oscillation
To allow for cooling in a wider area, the fan can oscillate within an arc when the pin is pressed. To avoid using an additional motor for the oscillation, the rotation of the fan driven by the motor is converted into a side-to-side motion. The idea is based on the **4-bar mechanism**.

![[Pasted image 20260516014331.png]]
Four bars are attached to each other by pins, which they can rotate about. The **fixed bar** is fixed in place, while the **crank** is driven by a motor. The **follower** and **coupler** are forced to move along with the crank. 

![[Pasted image 20260516014753.png|697]]
![[Pasted image 20260516014822.png|324]]![[Pasted image 20260516014841.png|366]]
In this configuration, the blue bar is fixed, while the yellow is the crank. This causes the green bar to sweep side-to-side in a fixed-length arc.
