### Prelude
During the mid-19th century, the Ottoman Empire has fallen behind the other European powers in terms of modernization and technology since the late 18th century, earning it the nickname "**the sick man of Europe**." Its weakness threatens the balance of power in Europe, a problem known as the **Eastern Question**, and results in several wars between the Ottomans and the Russians between the 1760s to 1820s, of which the Russians are mostly victorious. Now in the 1850s, Russia views itself as the protector and leader of Orthodox Christians, seeking to unite Constantinople and Central Asia under a shared Christian empire. Britain, France, and Austria want to limit Russian expansion while keeping the Ottoman's afloat, and so they offer assistance to the ailing empire, though with concessional demands.
### Escalation
A long-standing source of tension has been the status of the Christian minority in Ottoman territory. **Tsar Nicholas I** claimed that Russia was legally the guardian of Orthodox Christians, while **Emperor Napoleon III** of France represented Roman Catholics. In 1852, a fight between the two sides breaks out over who holds the keys to the **Church of the Nativity** in the Christian holy site of **Bethlehelm**, located in Ottoman-controlled Palestine. Napoleon III intervenes and sends the warship **Charlemagne** toward Constantinople to pressure the sultan **Abdulmejid I** into granting the Catholic Church supreme authority over the site, to which the sultan yields. In response, Russian envoy **Alexander Menshikov** arrives in Constantinople in February 1853 demanding that the decision be reversed. He then escalates the situation when he threatens to break off relations altogether on May 5th; the British and French encourage the Ottomans to stand their ground.

Ottoman politicians are split between those who want peace and seek compromise, and those seeking a war of resistance against Russia, though even those who seek peace support the decision to strengthen its military and seek allyship in Europe. After Saint Petersburg officially breaks diplomatic ties, the Ottomans begin mobilizing. Tensions remain high for months until finally the Russians make the first move, invading the **Danubian Principalities of Moldovia and Wallachia** (roughly modern day Romania), vassal states of the Ottoman Empire. Britain and France declare their support for the Ottomans, who then declare war on Russia on October 4th, 1853. Religion plays a large role in the war, arguably more than politics, with many calling it "the last crusade." 

Though Tsar Nicholas I insists the war is purely in the interest of the Christian Orthodoxy, it serves as a step towards their geopolitical goals: weaken the Ottoman Empire, annex its territory, and create a Russia-friendly Balkans, all to support its long-term goal of controlling the Turkish straights. The Ottomans are mainly concerned with preserving their empire and retaking the Danubian Principalities, along with establishing independent states in Georgia, Crimea, and Circassia with British and French support.

The Russian Empire fields the world's largest standing army at this time, with 900,000 regulars and 500,000 in reserve, though most of it is tied up maintaining supply lines and defending the empire's borders. Failure to modernize its military, the Ottoman army is poorly equipped and organized. They estimate about 105,000 regulars and 104,000 in reserve, but even they aren't completely sure on the numbers. They do possess modern western military. At sea, the Ottoman navy is much weaker than the Russians', who possess modern warships.
### Battle of Sinop
The first battle of the war occurs in November 1853 at sea. Russian warships make devastating use of newly-invented explosive shells in an attack against the Ottoman navy anchored at **Sinop**. With the sinking of 11 Ottoman ships, Russia gains naval supremacy over the Black Sea. French and British press frame the Russians in a negative light, calling it the "massacre at Sinop."
### Danube Campaign
Russia's first campaign on land begins along the **Danube River**. They believe that if they march into the Balkans, the local Christian majority might revolt against the Muslim Ottomans. Their initial attacks fail, however, as the Ottomans have dug in along the river.

![[Pasted image 20260615154130.png]]
The winter of 1853-54 is quiet, but the Russian offensive resumes in March 1854 as the Russians attack the Ottoman fortresses at **Vidin**, which stands in the way of Russian-aligned Serbia, and at the well-defended **Silistra**, which the Russians besiege. During this time, fighting also occurs in the Caucasus region, but little progress is gained as Ottoman victories are overturned by Russian counterattacks. In June, the Russians prepare to storm Silistra, but are called back just two hours before, as the war had now shifted in character.
### France and Britain Declare War
During the fighting, the Ottomans and Russians had been discussing peace in Vienna, but these eventually fall through, and France and Britain give an ultimatum to Russia demanding their withdrawal from the Danubian Principalities. When Russia refuses, Britain and France declare war on Russia in March of 1854. Britain seeks to block Russian expansion, prevent its control of the Straits, and protect its commercial interests. France joins the war in the name of prestige, extend its influence, protect commercial interests, and support national unification and independence movements of smaller people when convenient to French interests. The new alliance of Britain, France, and the Ottoman Empire now has the best military at its disposal. The French provide the bulk of the armed forces with its 439,000 men available, while the somewhat outdated British army only have 150,000 men, of which only 21,500 are available to aid the Ottomans. The combined force still struggles with inexperienced officers and issues in organization and supply, however the British and French do have access to mass-produced rifles that outrange those of the Russians. At sea, the allies have 60 ships-of-the-line against 40 Russian, but in total the allies boast 400 warships of all types, while the Russians have less than 100.

![[Pasted image 20260615155706.png]]
The allies promise to send 100,000 troops to the port of **Varna** to support the Ottomans at the Danube. Additionally, the Ottomans allow the Austrians, who fear that Russia's presence in the Balkans will encourage revolts among Slavic peoples in the Austrian Empire, to occupy the Danubian Principalities to force the Russians to withdraw. Habsburg Emperor **Franz Josef** mobilizes 280,000 troops demanding that the Russians leave. Fearing the destruction of its army, the Russians withdraw, alongside some Bulgarians who don't want to continue living under Ottoman rule.
### Crimean Campaign
![[Pasted image 20260615160902.png]]
Now with the Russians out of the Balkans, the allies now take their forces from Varna across the Black Sea to the Crimean peninsula, with the goal of capturing the Russian naval base at **Sevastopol**. There they face many logistical problems, such as the lack of reliable maps of the region, though commanders still expect a quick campaign. About 60,000 of the combined forces (though mainly French) land at **Yevpatoriia** on September 14, 1854. Many have already become plagued by cholera from their time in Varna, and the troops are forced to confiscate supplies from local Tatars due to lack of transport. Russia had not expected such a large landing so late in the season, and only have 25,000 men on the peninsula.
### Battle of the Alma
![[Pasted image 20260615161443.png]]
With the allies, under the command of **Lord Raglan** and **Marechal Jacques le Roy de Saint Arnaud**, now marching toward Sevastopol, Russian commander Prince Alexander Menshikov stations his army onto the heights overlooking the **Alma River** to block their advance. The allied attack begins on September 20th with the support of their coastal naval guns. Lord Raglan waits for the French to make their breakthrough first, exposing his men to artillery fire. The French are bogged down by the heavy fire of the Russians, and finally the British begin their assault, but they too are deterred by the Russians and are forced to retreat. The Russians capitalize on this and stage a counterattack, but are fended off by accurate British rifle fire. After the war, many British soldiers retroactively receive the **Victoria Cross** for their actions at the Alma.

![[Pasted image 20260615162356.png]]
Meanwhile, the Ottomans and French finally achieve a breakthrough on their side of the front, and an Allied victory soon follows. The battle was costly for both sides; in just three hours, the Allies had lost 3,600 men, with the Russians losing 5,000. Both sides also leave their wounded unattended.

Following the victory at Alma, the Allied leaders don't realize how weak the Russians are and delay their advance by two days before marching to the south side of Sevastopol, which is supposedly weaker. On the way, they take the port city of **Balaklava**, making its harbour their primary supply point. Most of the Russian troops retreat into the Peninsula's interior, leaving those at Sevastopol to be besieged by the Allies, who also set up camp and dig protective trenches and redoubts.
### Battle of Balaklava
![[Pasted image 20260615195543.png]]
In an attempt to sever the Allied supply route from the harbour to relieve the siege at Sevastopol, the Russians attack Balaklava on October 25th. At first, a small regiment of 500 Ottomans defending the first redoubt put up strong resistance, but are eventually driven out; their defeat breaks the moral of the other defenders, leaving just the British 93rd Division in defense of the harbour. With their powerful rifles, the British form a line and repel the Russians, as reporter **William Howard Russel** describes:

>"The Russians dash at the Highlanders. The ground flies beneath their horses' feet; gathering speed at every stride, they dash on towards that thin red streak topped with a line of steel."

With his words later misquoted, the defense would be forever remembered as the "**thin red line**."

With Allied reinforcements on the way, the Russians abandon their attack and take the Allied cannons with them, an act that hurts Lord Raglan's pride so much that he orders his troops to recapture the guns, but they are unsure as to which guns he refers to. In the confusion, the British **Light Cavalry Brigade** mistakenly charges down the valley straight into the teeth of a Russian artillery regiment. Their attack ends in failure and they are forced to retreat, with a third wounded or captured, and over a half dead. The attack would go down in infamy as the **Charge of the Light Brigade** as a symbol of officer incompetence. While the Russians technically win the battle, they had failed to reach the harbour.
### Battle of Inkerman
Emboldened by the victory at Balaklava, the Russians now look toward taking Inkerman Ridge, which would allow their artillery to fire into the rear of the Allied siege lines. Perceiving the British section of the line to be weaker, 50,000 Russians attack a defending force of only 16,000 Allied troops on November 5th. The battlefield is obscured by thick fog, resulting in a breakdown of Russian command and control. The battle deteriorates into a series of chaotic small-scale actions, with Russian troops taking redoubts only to lose them to British counterattacks. The Russian confusion also prevents planned diversionary attacks against the French, who now come to the aid of the British.

With the French reinforcements and confusion among Russian officers, the Russians are forced to retreat. The battle ends in a clear Allied victory, having successfully defended their position and inflicting 12,000 casualties on the Russians while only suffering 4,300 of their own. As winter draws in, the battle marks an indefinite end to any Russian attempts at lifting the siege at Sevastopol.
