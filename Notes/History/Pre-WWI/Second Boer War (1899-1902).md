### Prelude
Since the mid-17th century, Dutch settlers had established the **Cape Colony** settlement in what is now South Africa. They called themselves Afrikaners and spoke their own dialect of **Afrikaans**, but are more often referred to as **Boers**, meaning "peasant" in Dutch. They seized lands from and enslaved the natives of South Africa. After the Battle of Cape Town in 1806 during the Napoleonic Wars, Great Britain maintained permanent control over the colony after 1815, but conflicts over language, culture, and legislation arose between the Boers and the British, which came to a head in 1834 when Britain outlawed slavery.

This prompted a mass exodus of Boers known as the , who relied on slaves to run their farms, out of the colony to found new independent settlements in the east, an event known as the **Great Trek (1835-1840)**. There they founded two new republics: the **Orange Free State** and the **South African Republic (ZAR)**, where they continued the practice of slavery and stole land from local Africans.

![[Pasted image 20260531002554.png]]
As Britain expanded its territory, it came to share borders with the Boer republics. In 1887, the British announced their formal annexation of the ZAR and renamed it as the **Transvaal Colony**; the colony was economically weak after years of fighting its neighbors, and the British feared they would succumb to the Zulu or the Germans to the north. After three years of failed diplomacy by the local Boers to remove the British, the Transvaal Boers resorted to violence, resulting in the **First Boer War (1880-1881)**. The British were not prepared for the Boers' guerilla tactics, and believing that the colony wasn't worth the cost of fighting a drawn-out war, the British agreed to withdraw and grant them self-rule (aside from foreign policy), on the condition they would remain a part of the British Empire.

Then came the **Transvaal Gold Rush** from 1884 to 1886 after prospectors discovered huge deposits of gold, attracting immigrants from all over the British Empire. Soon British statesmen and businessmen started to pay attention to the region, including Cape Colony Prime Minister **Cecil Rhodes**, who led the campaign to bring the republics under British rule and secure their natural resources. However, he only managed to reveal to the Boers the threat of annexation by Great Britain when, in 1895, he conducted mercenaries to carry out the failed **Jameson Raid** on Boer territory. Regardless, he continued to pressure the Boer republics into granting legal and territorial concessions.
### Outbreak of War
The Boers, in fear of British aggression, began purchasing weapons from the Germans, an act that stoked British fears of German expansion. British troops began amassing in the nearby colony of **Natal**, and by 1899, it was clear to the Boers that invasion was imminent, and so decided to make the first move. On October 11th, 1899, the Boers made a preemptive strike against the British, marking the beginning of the **Second Boer War**.

The Boers did not have a professional military and relied on local militias. When the war began, the government conscripted all able-bodied men between the ages 16-60. While most were expected to bring their own rifle and horse, the government had also distributed the thousands of Mauser rifles that they had purchased from the Germans. While the Boers lacked any formal military training, they were formidable marksmen, having developed their skills hunting game and competing in shooting tournaments during peacetime. Every Boer was skilled as a mounted rifleman, able to move around quickly to take key positions, before dismounting and firing their rifles with great accuracy. Several decades later, Winston Churchill would name the British special forces "commandos," after the Afrikaans word for military unit. The Boers also possessed modern German and French artillery guns.

While they were well-armed, skilled, motivated to fight for independence and 60,000 strong, they were highly dependent on imports from Europe to support their large population, and could not afford a long war of attrition, and feared any opportunities of an African uprising or invasion by the Zulus. Hence, their goal was to score a quick, decisive victory against the British.

The British not only could field much greater numbers than the Boers (with 250,000 troops available), but were well experienced in colonial warfare, having won many conflicts in the past by taking advantage of their superior technology, military discipline, and tactics based on tight formations and concentrated barrages. However, they lacked experience in the face of the modern rifles and artillery fielded by the Boers, something they overlooked to their detriment. Despite their defeat to the Transvaal Boers in 1881, British military command believed the war would be won swiftly and decisively, expecting the Boers to be weak and undisciplined. Native Africans were employed by both sides during the war; the Boers used African slave labour to transport supplies and dig trenches, while the British hired thousands of Africans to help carry out logistics, tracking, and scouting.
### The Boer Offensive
![[Pasted image 20260601180713.png]]
In their initial strike, the Boers attacked the British on four fronts: the railway town of **Mafeking**, the diamond-mining town of **Kimberley**, the Midland Region of Cape Colony to disrupt rail transport, and into the Natal colony, where the main Boer offensive took place. This is where the bulk of the British forces were (about 15,000 troops, at this time the Boers outnumbered the British, with 40,000 Boers vs 20,000 British), as they planned to use it as a staging ground for their invasion of the Boer colonies.
#### Battle of Talana Hill
![[Pasted image 20260601181423.png]]
At Natal, the British were poorly deployed, and on October 20th, the Boers took to **Talana Hill** and bombarded a detachment of 4000 men. While the British were able to eventually drive the Boers off with a cavalry charge, they suffered heavy casualties, and were then in danger of being cut off from the main British garrison at **Ladysmith**. Though they faced attacks from the mobile Boer units during their retreat, they were able to defeat the Boers through skilled coordination and the use of cavalry.
#### Battle of Ladysmith
By the end of October, the Boer advance had not slowed, and British commander **George White**, was faced with making the decision between retreating to preserve his army, or attack the Boers head-on and attempt to turn the tide of the war in a decisive victory. He chose the latter, attacking the Boers in a full frontal assault on October 30th at the Battle of Ladysmith. The British were uncoordinated, however, and White was forced to retreat to Ladysmith, where they were then besieged by the Boers.
