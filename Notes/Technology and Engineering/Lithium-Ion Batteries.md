All batteries have a negative terminal (**anode**) and a positive terminal (**cathode**). Negatively-charged electrons flow from the anode to the cathode. In a **lithium-ion battery**, electrons come from the element lithium. 
### The Anode
At the anode, lithium is stored between layers of carbon graphite. Graphite has a crystal structure of layered planes that allows lithium to be intercalated, or wedged in between the layers. The graphite provides a stable space for storing the lithium. As lithium's atomic number is 3, it has one unstable valence electron. When there is an available path from the anode to the cathode, the electron separates from the lithium and travels to the other terminal. At the same time, the lithium becomes a +1 positively-charged ion and leaves the graphite lattice. When a lot of lithium ions leave at the same time, a flow of electrons is produced, travelling through and powering the device's circuits and components on their way to the cathode.
### The Cathode
In batteries used in personal consumer electronics, like smartphones, tablets, and cameras, limited space is available. **Lithium Cobalt Oxide** is used for its high specific energy, meaning it can store a lot of power while remaining small and lightweight. The tradeoff is that it has a relatively shorter lifespan, lower thermal stability, and is more expensive when compared to other cathode materials.

In the cathode is a lattice of cobalt and oxygen. The cobalt, having lost its electrons to the oxygen, are positively +4 charged, and are very willing to accept electrons. Since lithium wants to lose electrons, electrons from the anode travel to the cathode. As more and more electrons flow in one direction, the cathode begins to become more negatively-charged, while the anode becomes more and more positively-charged, which would stifle the flow of electrons in the right direction. To rectify this, an **electrolyte** provides a path for the now positive lithium ions to migrate from the anode to the cathode, while preventing the flow of electrons through it. When it reaches the cathode, lithium intercalates with the cobalt and oxygen to become Lithium Cobalt Oxide. The addition of positive lithium ions balances the charge. 
### Recharging
At the end of the battery's lifespan the electrons and lithium from the anode will have all moved to the other end, and so the battery's charge will be empty.
